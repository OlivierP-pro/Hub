2.1Online Uncertainty Quantification for a Drone's Return-to-Home Decision
100% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

A drone triggers its automatic return when the remaining energy no longer covers the predicted return energy plus a fixed safety margin; read as a forecast, this margin has the same spread for every flight. We build instead a flight-conditional predictive law of the battery charge at arrival and set the margin from a chosen risk level. The prediction loop has exactly five random inputs, each modelled as μ + Fξ with a Gaussian germ frozen over the return. Candidate uncertainty models are ranked with the CRPS, a strictly proper scoring rule, through a logarithmic skill score that splits exactly into a centre and a width term, the latter bounded by 0.2256. On board, uncertainty is propagated by an unscented transform and by a Hermite polynomial chaos whose coefficients are learnt online by a Kalman filter, with a three-term bound on the error of the decision quantile. Under a mean-survival criterion, the value of conditional uncertainty is shown to be of second order, positive only beyond z = √2 or with conditional bias correction.

Keywords: uncertainty quantification, proper scoring rules, CRPS, polynomial chaos, unscented transform, Kalman filter.

2.2Inferring Audience Interests with BERT for Ad Targeting
50% completed5 clients[PDF, 2 pages]

Advertisers usually target what a video is about rather than who watches it. We infer the interests of a channel's audience from the public comments it receives. Each comment is mapped to the unit sphere by a contrastively fine-tuned BERT encoder, and a channel is represented by the kernel mean embedding of its comment distribution. Under a topic-mixture model, this embedding is a linear image of the audience's average interest vector; its inner product with an advertiser's description is the expected affinity of a random commenter with the product, and its estimator concentrates at a dimension-free rate. Look-alike targeting reduces to a maximum mean discrepancy between audiences. On 3,154 French YouTube channels, the method recovers channels already sponsored by a given advertiser with AUC 0.87, an 18× lift over random in the top 20, without any individual-level profiling.

Keywords: BERT, sentence embeddings, kernel mean embedding, maximum mean discrepancy, audience targeting.

2.3The log S-fBM Model: a Stationary Bridge between Rough and Multifractal Volatility
20% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

Empirical volatility is rough: its logarithm behaves like a fractional Brownian motion with Hurst exponent H ≈ 0.1 or smaller, while multifractal random measures (MRM) describe the limit H → 0 but have no roughness parameter. The log stationary fractional Brownian motion (log S-fBM) contains both. We prove that its covariance is admissible, show its convergence to the log-normal MRM as H → 0 at fixed intermittency, and derive exact second-order moments of the integrated variance and of its logarithm. These moments define a generalised method of moments (GMM) estimator that avoids the upward bias of scaling-based estimators of H, observed in simulation where a true H = 0.002 is estimated at 0.082.

Keywords: rough volatility, multifractal random measure, fractional Brownian motion, generalised method of moments.

2.4End-to-End Verifiable Online Voting without a Blockchain
20% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

More than 77% of French voters abroad voted online in the 2022 legislative elections, yet none could verify that their ballot had been counted. We describe an end-to-end verifiable protocol in the line of Helios that requires no blockchain. Ballots are encrypted with exponential ElGamal, so that the tally is computed on ciphertexts; each ballot carries non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs of validity; the decryption key is shared among trustees; every voter can find their ballot on a public bulletin board and anyone can recompute the result. We state the security reductions, prove the soundness and zero-knowledge property of the proofs, and show that universal verification of an election with 106 voters takes minutes on commodity hardware, without the energy cost of a public blockchain.

Keywords: homomorphic encryption, zero-knowledge proofs, threshold decryption, verifiable voting.

2.5Signature Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures: a Rough Path Formalisation of Latent Trajectory Prediction in World Models
10% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

Joint-embedding predictive architectures (JEPA) learn a world model by predicting the representation of a hidden part of a signal from the representation of a visible part, rather than the signal itself. When the signal is a trajectory (a video, a controlled robot, a physical system), what should the representation of a time window be? We formalise JEPA as a tuple of encoders, a predictor and a masking law, and show that rough path theory supplies a canonical answer: the truncated signature of a learnt latent path. This embedding is compositional (Chen’s identity turns temporal masking into tensor products), universal (continuous functionals of the window are linear in it), consistent under change of sampling rate, and, through the expected signature, characterises laws of trajectories. We prove that the L2-optimal predictor is the conditional expected signature; that it determines the conditional law of the future and makes the expected cost of any continuous objective a linear form, which is what planning requires; that a predictor valued in the free nilpotent Lie group yields multi-step rollouts with an explicit error bound; and that a non-learnt anchor channel excludes representational collapse. A stochastic version is trained with a strictly proper scoring rule built on the signature kernel.

Keywords: rough paths, path signatures, JEPA, world models, expected signature, signature kernel, free Lie algebras, self-supervised learning.

2.6Wafer Yield as a Functional of a Marked Cox Process: Critical Area, Defect Clustering and Hierarchical Spatial Inference
100% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

We formulate die yield as the void probability of a marked point process of defects restricted to the critical region of a layout. Under a Poisson law this gives the exact model Y = e−λ, where λ is the defect density times the size-averaged critical area, not the die area. Clustering is modelled by a Cox process: yield becomes a Laplace functional, which contains the Murphy, Seeds and negative binomial models as particular mixing laws and is never below the Poisson yield. We show that the clustering parameter is not intrinsic: it depends on the scale of the mixing relative to the die, and only large-scale mixing correlates neighbouring dies. A log-Gaussian Cox model with lot, wafer, radial and Matérn spatial effects plus a systematic component leads to a complementary log-log mixed model, derived rather than assumed. We give identifiability conditions, the law of good dies per wafer, defect-tolerant yield and a Bayesian inference procedure.

Keywords: yield modelling, critical area, Cox processes, negative binomial, log-Gaussian Cox process, spatial statistics, INLA.

2.7List Scheduling of Dependent Non-Preemptive Tasks
100% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

We minimise the makespan of tasks with precedence constraints on m identical machines without preemption, a strongly NP-hard problem. List scheduling guarantees a ratio 2 − 1/m for any priority rule (Graham); we compare the height, depth, t-level, b-level, number-of-successors and duration rules, a fuzzy tie-breaking rule, and a genetic algorithm searching over weighted combinations of these priorities. On 180 random graphs of 1,000 tasks, the b-level rule reduces the makespan by 2.43% on average relative to the height rule (standard error 0.30); neither the fuzzy tie-break nor the genetic algorithm yields a significant further gain, which makes b-level list scheduling the best rule at equal computational cost.

Keywords: P|prec|Cmax, list scheduling, critical path, b-level, genetic algorithms.

2.8Bayesian Poisson Regression for Defect Prediction from Storage Time
100% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

With about a hundred products, we model the number of defects as a function of storage time by a Bayesian Poisson regression with log link. The posterior is strictly log-concave, which justifies Laplace and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo inference. The naive quantity P(β1 ≠ 0 | data) equals 1 under any continuous prior; we replace it by directional probabilities, a region of practical equivalence and a Savage–Dickey Bayes factor. Rao–Blackwellised estimators give the probability that a product falls into each defect band; these probabilities increase with storage time, which yields a maximal storage time at a chosen risk level. Conditions for a causal reading and model checks (overdispersion, WAIC, leave-one-out cross-validation) are made explicit.

Keywords: Bayesian inference, Poisson regression, Hamiltonian Monte Carlo, Bayes factors, predictive risk.

2.9An Automated Pipeline for Short-Form Video: Speech Recognition, Captions, Music Generation and Distribution
100% completed[PDF, 2 pages]

We describe the mathematics of a pipeline that turns a raw video into a captioned, scored video published on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram. Speech is transcribed by Whisper, an encoder–decoder Transformer on log-Mel spectrograms; word timestamps are obtained by dynamic time warping on cross-attention; captions are formed by an optimal segmentation of the word sequence and highlighted word by word without timing drift. Background music is sampled from MusicGen, an autoregressive Transformer over residual-vector-quantised audio codes with classifier-free guidance, conditioned on the transcript and mixed at a target loudness. Publication is formulated as an assignment problem under API quotas.

Keywords: speech recognition, dynamic time warping, dynamic programming, neural audio codecs, music generation.
